An ecosystem has living (biotic) parts like plants and animals, and nonliving (abiotic) parts like sunlight, water, soil and temperature.

NC objective LS.5.2.1: Engage in argument from evidence to compare the characteristics of several common ecosystems (including estuaries and salt marshes, oceans, lakes and ponds, rivers and streams, forests, and grasslands) in terms of their ability to support a variety of populations.

I can use evidence to compare ecosystems and explain why organisms live only where their needs are met.

Big idea

An ecosystem has living (biotic) parts like plants and animals, and nonliving (abiotic) parts like sunlight, water, soil and temperature. Organisms survive only where their needs are met. North Carolina has them all: mountain forests, piedmont streams, coastal estuaries and salt marshes, and the ocean.

Key ideas
  • Biotic factors: plants, animals, other living things.
  • Abiotic factors: temperature, sunlight, water, soil, ocean currents.
  • Terrestrial ecosystems: forest, grassland, desert. Aquatic: ocean, lake, pond, river, stream, estuary, salt marsh.
  • A healthy ecosystem has many species meeting their needs in a stable web of life.
  • Newly introduced species can damage the balance of an ecosystem.

Science words

Click a word to see what it means.

ecosystem

Living and nonliving things interacting in an area.

biotic

Living, or once living.

abiotic

Nonliving, like water or sunlight.

estuary

Where a river meets the sea and fresh water mixes with salt water.

salt marsh

A coastal wetland with grasses that flood with the tides.

population

All the members of one species in an area.

habitat

The place an organism lives.

terrestrial / aquatic

On land / in water.

Quick check

Answer each question in your head or notebook, then click Check answer.

1. Which is an ABIOTIC factor?

  1. A salt marsh grass
  2. Water temperature
  3. A blue crab
Check answer

B. Water temperature: Temperature isn't alive.

2. Where does fresh river water mix with salty ocean water?

  1. A grassland
  2. An estuary
  3. A desert
Check answer

B. An estuary: That mix is what makes an estuary.

3. Which claim does the evidence best support?

  1. Organisms can live anywhere
  2. Organisms survive only where their needs are met
  3. Only plants need water
Check answer

B. Organisms survive only where their needs are met: Each population needs the right food, water, shelter and temperature.
