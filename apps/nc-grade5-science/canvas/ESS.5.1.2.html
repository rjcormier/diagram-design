ESS.5.1 · Earth's Systems · ESS.5.1.2

Meteorologists measure many things to forecast weather.

NC objective ESS.5.1.2: Analyze and interpret weather data to explain current and upcoming weather conditions (including severe weather such as hurricanes and tornadoes) in a given location.

I can use air pressure, fronts and clouds to predict upcoming weather, including severe storms.

Big idea

Meteorologists measure many things to forecast weather. High pressure usually brings clear skies. Low pressure brings clouds, wind and precipitation. When a cold front pushes into warm air, warm air rises and storms can form. Hurricanes form over warm ocean water with low pressure and very high winds.

Key ideas
  • Air masses form over land or ocean and are warm or cold, moist or dry.
  • Fronts are where air masses meet. Cold fronts can bring heavy rain and thunderstorms.
  • Falling barometer = low pressure coming = unsettled weather.
  • Warm air rises; cold air sinks.
  • Tornadoes: thunderstorms, strong winds, rotation. Hurricanes: warm ocean, low pressure, high winds.
  • Maps, radar and satellites track storms. (You don't need to name cloud types.)

Math connection: Eureka Math²

Barometers read in decimals (like 29.92 inches). Comparing and subtracting decimals shows whether pressure is falling, and how fast.

  • Module 4: Place Value Concepts for Decimal Operations

NC.5.NBT.3 compare decimals to thousandths
NC.5.NBT.7 subtract decimals

Worked example: Monday 30.21 in. → Thursday 29.58 in. 30.21 − 29.58 = 0.63 in. drop. Falling pressure means a storm may be coming.

Science words

Click a word to see what it means.

air mass

A huge body of air with similar temperature and moisture.

front

The boundary where two air masses meet.

air pressure

The weight of air pressing down.

forecast

A prediction of upcoming weather.

radar

A tool that shows where precipitation is.

hurricane

A huge storm that forms over warm ocean water.

tornado

A rotating column of air touching the ground.

severe weather

Dangerous weather like storms and hail.

1. The barometer is falling quickly. What weather is most likely coming?

  1. Clear and sunny
  2. Clouds, wind and precipitation
Check answer

B. Clouds, wind and precipitation: Low pressure brings unsettled weather.

2. Which conditions help a hurricane form?

  1. Cold land and high pressure
  2. Warm ocean water, low pressure, high winds
  3. Dry desert air
Check answer

B. Warm ocean water, low pressure, high winds: Hurricanes need warm water as fuel.

3. What usually happens when a cold front meets a warm, moist air mass?

  1. Heavy precipitation, possible thunderstorms
  2. Nothing
  3. It gets foggy forever
Check answer

A. Heavy precipitation, possible thunderstorms: Warm air is pushed up, cools, and forms storm clouds.
