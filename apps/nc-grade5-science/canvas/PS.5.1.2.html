PS.5.1 · Matter and Its Interactions · PS.5.1.2

Some changes only change shape or state, like dissolving sugar or crushing a can.

NC objective PS.5.1.2: Carry out investigations to explain whether the mixing of two or more substances results in new substances.

I can look for evidence (color change, odor, bubbles, temperature change) to decide whether mixing made a new substance.

Big idea

Some changes only change shape or state, like dissolving sugar or crushing a can. Other changes make a brand-new substance with different properties, like rust on a nail or bubbles from baking soda and vinegar. Either way, the total weight does not change.

Key ideas
  • Physical change: same substance, new shape or state (melting, dissolving, crushing).
  • New substance: properties change. Look for color change, new odor, gas bubbles, or temperature change.
  • Record observations in a before-and-after chart.
  • No matter what change happens, the total weight of the substances stays the same.

Science words

Click a word to see what it means.

physical change

A change in size, shape or state. No new substance forms.

chemical change

A change that makes a new substance with new properties.

properties

Traits like color, smell, texture, weight.

qualitative data

Data you describe in words (it turned blue).

quantitative data

Data you measure with numbers (it weighs 12 g).

variable

A factor that can change in an experiment.

control

The part of a test you keep the same to compare.

mass

The amount of matter in an object.

Talk like a scientist

  • What are the quantitative and qualitative properties of ________?
  • What is the result of combining the two substances?

Quick check

Answer each question in your head or notebook, then click Check answer.

1. Which observation is the best evidence that a NEW substance formed?

  1. The sugar disappeared in the water
  2. Bubbles of gas formed and a balloon filled up
  3. The can got smaller when crushed
Check answer

B. Bubbles of gas formed and a balloon filled up: Formation of a gas is a clue that a new substance formed.

2. Which change does NOT make a new substance?

  1. A nail rusting
  2. Bread burning
  3. Ice melting
Check answer

C. Ice melting: Melting is a change of state. It is still water.

3. 'The mixture weighs 186.4 g' is what kind of data?

  1. Quantitative
  2. Qualitative
Check answer

A. Quantitative: It is measured with a number.
