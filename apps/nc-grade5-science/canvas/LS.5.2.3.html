LS.5.2 · Ecosystems: Interactions, Energy, and Dynamics · LS.5.2.3

Every organism in a food chain depends on the others.

NC objective LS.5.2.3: Use models to infer the effects that may result from the interconnected relationships of plants and animals to their ecosystem.

I can predict what happens to other populations when one part of a food chain changes.

Big idea

Every organism in a food chain depends on the others. If the grass dries up, grasshoppers lose food, then snakes lose food, then hawks. If one population grows, the ones that eat it may grow too. Models help us predict these changes.

Key ideas
  • Organisms survive only where their needs are met.
  • A change to one population ripples through the food chain.
  • If a food source disappears, consumers must move, find new food, or die.
  • Patterns in population data help us make predictions.

Quick check

Answer each question in your head or notebook, then click Check answer.

1. In grass → grasshopper → snake → hawk, grasshoppers suddenly increase. What happens first?

  1. Grass decreases and snakes have more food
  2. Hawks disappear
  3. Nothing changes
Check answer

A. Grass decreases and snakes have more food: More grasshoppers eat more grass and feed more snakes.

2. Wild berries that cardinals eat become scarce. What happens to the cardinal population?

  1. It increases
  2. It likely decreases
  3. It stays exactly the same
Check answer

B. It likely decreases: Less food means fewer cardinals can survive.

3. A consumer's only food source disappears. Which is NOT a likely result?

  1. It moves to find food
  2. It finds a new food source
  3. It makes its own food from sunlight
Check answer

C. It makes its own food from sunlight: Only producers make food from sunlight.
